Owning the Build: How Modal Overlays and Event Bubbling Actually Work

The line between “I built this” and “AI built something I can't explain” is the credibility test. Here is my plain-words breakdown of the mystery code behind our portfolio's interactive popup modals.

1. The Mystery Code in index.html

When AI generated the booking and CV modals for my portfolio, it included a line that caught my attention: 

<!-- Full-screen dark overlay -->
<div id="booking-modal" class="modal-overlay" onclick="closeModal('booking-modal')">

  <!-- The popup card sitting inside -->
  <div class="modal-card" onclick="event.stopPropagation()">
    <button class="modal-close" onclick="closeModal('booking-modal')">&times;</button>
    <h3>Schedule a Technical Chat</h3>
    <p>Select a time to discuss AI agent design...</p>
  </div>

</div>
The Question I Asked:
Why do we need onclick="event.stopPropagation()" on the inner card? What is propagating, and why would clicking inside the white box close the window if that line was missing?

2. Plain-Words Explanation (Teaching a Friend)

Imagine you have two clear plastic sheets stacked on top of each other:

1. The Tinted Sheet (Overlay)

A dark, semi-transparent sheet that covers the whole screen. When you click this dark space, you want the popup to disappear.

2. The White Card (Dialog)

A solid white card resting right in the middle containing your text, links, and booking buttons.

How Browsers Notice Clicks (Event Bubbling):
When you click something on a web page, the browser doesn't just register the click at that exact pixel. It treats the click like dropping a pebble into a pond—the ripples travel outward and upward through every parent box that wraps around that element.

Because the white card lives inside the dark background in our code, clicking a button inside the card causes the click ripple to bubble upward until it touches the dark background.

Without event.stopPropagation(), the dark background hears that a click happened and immediately triggers closeModal()—abruptly slamming the popup shut in the user's face while they are trying to read or type!

What event.stopPropagation() does:
It tells the browser: “Handle whatever the user clicked right here inside this white box, and STOP the ripple from traveling up to the dark background.”

3. How the CSS Creates the Dimmed Effect

Three key CSS rules work together on .modal-overlay to make this feel like a native application: 

.modal-overlay {
  position: fixed;                    /* Pins the layer to the screen regardless of scrolling */
  top: 0; left: 0; width: 100%; height: 100%; /* Stretches across the entire viewport */
  background: rgba(9, 13, 22, 0.85);  /* 85% opacity dark slate tint */
  backdrop-filter: blur(8px);         /* Blurs the portfolio behind the modal */
  z-index: 1000;                      /* Forces the modal to sit on top of all page elements */
}
